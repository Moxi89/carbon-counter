# Global Carbon Budget 2026: release-day runbook

The Global Carbon Project publishes its yearly budget in November, usually during the UN climate
conference (COP31, Antalya, 9–20 November 2026). Searches for "CO₂ emissions 2026" peak that week, so the
goal is to have the new figures live **within a day of the release**.

Everything below is prepared and was practised on 23 October 2026 with made-up numbers.

| Step | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 min | Collect the figures into `tools/yearly/gcb.json` |
| 2 | 10 min | Preview and apply the site-wide update |
| 3 | 45–60 min | Write the key-findings post from the draft |
| 4 | 20 min | Check the hand-review list |
| 5 | 10 min | Rebuild, check, PR |
| 6 | later | Country data, when Our World in Data updates |

## 1. Collect the figures

Sources, in order of preference:

- Global Carbon Project press release and key messages: https://globalcarbonbudget.org/
- The paper (first as a preprint): https://essd.copernicus.org/ (search "Global Carbon Budget 2026")
- The Global Carbon Budget FAQs page for the new edition

Fill the `next` block in `tools/yearly/gcb.json`:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| `total_gt` | Total CO₂ emissions for 2026, fossil + land-use change, in Gt CO₂ (one decimal) |
| `fossil_gt`, `fossil_change_pct` | Fossil CO₂ (incl. cement) for 2026 and its % change on 2025 (negative if it fell) |
| `landuse_gt` | Land-use change CO₂ for 2026 |
| `ppm`, `ppm_above_preindustrial_pct` | Projected average atmospheric CO₂ for 2026 and % above pre-industrial |
| `budget_15_gt` | Remaining 1.5 °C budget, **50% likelihood**, in Gt CO₂ |
| `budget_start_year` | Year the budget counts from (expected 2027, i.e. from 1 January 2027): check it |
| `paper_url` | Link to the ESSD paper or preprint |

Then the `post` block (only used by the key-findings post): release date (YYYY-MM-DD), launch event, last
year's ppm, land-use and fuel changes (%), country changes (%), ocean and land sink shares (%) and their
period, 1.7 °C and 2 °C budgets, and the press release and FAQ links.

**Watch out:** the report gives some figures in GtC (carbon). The site uses Gt **CO₂**: 1 GtC = 3.664 Gt CO₂.
Use the 50% budget, not 67% or 83%.

## 2. Preview and apply

```sh
python3 tools/yearly/gcb_update.py            # dry run: every derived figure and every change, line by line
python3 tools/yearly/gcb_update.py --apply
```

The script works out per second, per minute, per hour, per day, years left, run-out date and the fossil /
land-use split from the headline figures, so they always agree. It updates the pages that show the live
figures: home page (text, key figures, FAQ, structured data), methodology, counter script, widget and embed,
footer, country-page generator, share-image text and the core figures table in `CONTENT_GUIDE.md`. It bumps the
`?v=` of `home-2026.js` and the footer, and rolls `gcb.json` forward so next year starts from the new figures.

Blog posts are **not** edited automatically: many describe the 2025 edition on purpose. The script lists the
lines to check instead (step 4).

## 3. Write the key-findings post

The draft is `tools/blog/drafts/global-carbon-budget-2026.html` (not built). Numbers are filled in
automatically; replace each `[[WRITE: ...]]` note with text from the report. Don't call anything a record, a
peak or a fall until the report says so. Then:

```sh
python3 tools/yearly/gcb_update.py --draft    # fills the numbers, refuses while notes or values are missing
```

It writes `tools/blog/content/global-carbon-budget-2026.html`. Country pages link to it after step 2, so publish
both in the same PR. Add a short callout at the top of the 2025 post (`critical-climate-year-2025`) pointing to
the new one.

## 4. Check the hand-review list

`--apply` prints the lines that still mention old figures. Check at least:

- **"Record" claims** on the home page and methodology: still true for 2026?
- **The other budget estimate** (Indicators of Global Climate Change, 130 Gt from 2026): is there a newer one?
  The home FAQ says "closer to three years"; adjust if needed.
- **The paper citation year** on the methodology page.
- **Blog posts**, most important first: `carbon-budget-explained`, `co2-ppm-atmosphere`, `2026-climate-outlook`,
  `net-zero-explained`, `beyond-2025-next-decade-climate`. Update "current" figures; leave historical ones.
  Set `updated` in each post you change.

## 5. Rebuild, check, PR

```sh
python3 tools/countries/build.py
python3 tools/blog/build.py
python3 tools/pages/build.py
python3 tools/ads/apply.py
python3 tools/og/jobs.py > /tmp/og.json && node tools/og/render.js /tmp/og.json
python3 tools/blog/images.py
```

Check the home counter and budget countdown, `/methodology`, `/widget` (both widgets), a country page and the
new post on a phone. Then open the PR. After merging: request indexing for the home page and the new post in
Search Console.

## 6. Country data (when Our World in Data updates)

Our World in Data usually updates its CO₂ dataset a few days to weeks after the release.

```sh
curl -LO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/owid/co2-data/master/owid-co2-data.csv
python3 tools/countries/build.py --csv owid-co2-data.csv
```

Then update by hand: "Global Carbon Budget 2025" in the two data credits in `tools/countries/build.py` (search for
`Global Carbon Project`), the country figures in the home FAQ (largest emitters, per person, historical), the
"China per second" row on the methodology page, and the per-person and top-emitter rows in `CONTENT_GUIDE.md`.
Rebuild as in step 5.

## Practice run (23 October 2026)

Made-up figures (42.9 Gt total, 130 Gt budget from 2027) produced 157 correct changes across 9 files, plus
the cache-version bumps, with no wrong replacements. The draft filled, built and rendered with no leftover
placeholders. All test changes were discarded.
