Questions and answers

Which country emits the most CO₂?

China emits the most CO₂: 12.29 billion tonnes in 2024, 31.8% of the world total. The United States (4.90 billion tonnes) and India (3.19 billion tonnes) follow. Together the top three produce 53% of global fossil CO₂.

Which country has the highest CO₂ emissions per person?

Among countries with more than one million people, Qatar has the highest emissions per person at 41.3 tonnes, followed by Kuwait (26.2 t) and Bahrain (24.3 t). The world average is 4.7 tonnes.

Which countries have emitted the most CO₂ in history?

Since 1750, the United States has emitted the most fossil CO₂ (23.5% of the total), followed by China (15.4%) and Russia (6.6%).

How much CO₂ does the world emit per year?

Fossil fuels and industry released 38.60 billion tonnes of CO₂ in 2024, about 4.7 tonnes per person. Including land-use change such as deforestation, the Global Carbon Budget 2025 puts total emissions at about 42.2 billion tonnes a year.

Where does this data come from?

Country figures come from Our World in Data, based on the Global Carbon Project’s Global Carbon Budget. They cover CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry (coal, oil, gas, cement, flaring) produced within each country’s borders.
