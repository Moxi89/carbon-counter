Turkey in context

1.1% vs 1.3%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Turkey is home to 1.1% of the world’s population and produces 1.3% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

+232%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 232% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (154.6 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

483 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Turkey was responsible for 483.0 Mt in 2023, about the same as the 486.9 Mt produced within its borders.

245 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Turkey came with about 245 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

0.19 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.19 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 35% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Turkey emit?

Turkey emitted 513.0 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 1.3% of global emissions. That is about 16 tonnes every second.

What are Turkey’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Turkey emits 5.9 tonnes per person, 1.2× the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (87.5 million people in 2024).

Are Turkey’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 5.4% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 92%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Turkey?

Coal is the largest source, at 41% of Turkey’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (209.3 million tonnes).

What are Turkey’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 483.0 million tonnes in 2023, roughly the same as its production-based emissions of 486.9 million tonnes. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Turkey contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Turkey has emitted about 13 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.7% of the world total, the 24th largest historical contribution.
