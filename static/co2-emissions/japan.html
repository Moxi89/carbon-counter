Japan in context

1.5% vs 2.5%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Japan is home to 1.5% of the world’s population and produces 2.5% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

−17%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 17% lower than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (1,154.9 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

1,142 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Japan was responsible for 1,142.3 Mt in 2023, 16% more than the 986.9 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

201 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Japan came with about 201 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a cleaner energy mix than the world average.

0.22 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.22 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 25% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Japan emit?

Japan emitted 961.9 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 2.5% of global emissions. That is about 31 tonnes every second.

What are Japan’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Japan emits 7.8 tonnes per person, 1.6× the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (123.8 million people in 2024).

Are Japan’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions fell 2.5% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are down 25%. Emissions peaked in 2013 at 1.31 billion tonnes and are now 27% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Japan?

Coal is the largest source, at 40% of Japan’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (381.0 million tonnes).

What are Japan’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 1,142.3 million tonnes in 2023, 16% more than the 986.9 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Japan contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Japan has emitted about 70 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 3.8% of the world total, the 6th largest historical contribution.
