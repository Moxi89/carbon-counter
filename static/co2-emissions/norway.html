Norway in context

0.07% vs 0.10%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Norway is home to 0.07% of the world’s population and produces 0.10% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

+6%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 6% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (35.0 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

39 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Norway was responsible for 39.0 Mt in 2023, about the same as the 38.9 Mt produced within its borders.

67 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Norway came with about 67 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much cleaner energy mix than the world average.

0.09 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.09 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 70% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Norway emit?

Norway emitted 37.2 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.1% of global emissions. That is about 1 tonnes every second.

What are Norway’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Norway emits 6.7 tonnes per person, 1.4× the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (5.6 million people in 2024).

Are Norway’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions fell 4.4% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are down 14%. Emissions peaked in 2010 at 45.7 million tonnes and are now 19% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Norway?

Oil is the largest source, at 56% of Norway’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (20.8 million tonnes).

What are Norway’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 39.0 million tonnes in 2023, roughly the same as its production-based emissions of 38.9 million tonnes. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Norway contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Norway has emitted about 3 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.1% of the world total, the 57th largest historical contribution.
