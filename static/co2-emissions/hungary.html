Hungary in context

0.12% vs 0.10%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Hungary is home to 0.12% of the world’s population and produces 0.10% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 46% lower than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (73.4 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

57 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Hungary was responsible for 56.7 Mt in 2023, 41% more than the 40.3 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Hungary emit?

Hungary emitted 40.0 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.1% of global emissions. That is about 1 tonnes every second.

What are Hungary’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Hungary emits 4.1 tonnes per person, 12% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (9.7 million people in 2024).

Are Hungary’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions fell 0.7% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are down 33%. Emissions peaked in 1984 at 90.7 million tonnes and are now 56% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Hungary?

Oil is the largest source, at 47% of Hungary’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (18.6 million tonnes).

What are Hungary’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 56.7 million tonnes in 2023, 41% more than the 40.3 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Hungary contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Hungary has emitted about 5 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.3% of the world total, the 43rd largest historical contribution.
