Ecuador in context

0.22% vs 0.12%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Ecuador is home to 0.22% of the world’s population and produces 0.12% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+179%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 179% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (16.5 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

49 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Ecuador was responsible for 48.9 Mt in 2023, 10% more than the 44.6 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

199 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Ecuador came with about 199 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a cleaner energy mix than the world average.

Its economy emitted 0.24 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 17% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Ecuador emit?

Ecuador emitted 46.0 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.1% of global emissions. That is about 1 tonnes every second.

What are Ecuador’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Ecuador emits 2.5 tonnes per person, 46% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (18.1 million people in 2024).

Are Ecuador’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 3.1% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 50%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Ecuador?

Oil is the largest source, at 86% of Ecuador’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (39.5 million tonnes).

What are Ecuador’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 48.9 million tonnes in 2023, 10% more than the 44.6 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Ecuador contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Ecuador has emitted about 1 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.1% of the world total, the 77th largest historical contribution.
