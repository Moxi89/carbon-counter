Brazil in context

2.6% vs 1.3%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Brazil is home to 2.6% of the world’s population and produces 1.3% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+121%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 121% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (218.7 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

470 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Brazil was responsible for 470.0 Mt in 2023, about the same as the 484.0 Mt produced within its borders.

123 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Brazil came with about 123 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much cleaner energy mix than the world average.

0.15 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.15 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 48% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Brazil emit?

Brazil emitted 483.0 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 1.2% of global emissions. That is about 15 tonnes every second.

What are Brazil’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Brazil emits 2.3 tonnes per person, 52% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (212.0 million people in 2024).

Are Brazil’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions were roughly flat in 2024 (-0.2% on 2023). Compared with 2005, emissions are up 33%. Emissions peaked in 2014 at 556.5 million tonnes and are now 13% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Brazil?

Oil is the largest source, at 67% of Brazil’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (323.9 million tonnes).

What are Brazil’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 470.0 million tonnes in 2023, roughly the same as its production-based emissions of 484.0 million tonnes. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Brazil contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Brazil has emitted about 18 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 1.0% of the world total, the 19th largest historical contribution.
