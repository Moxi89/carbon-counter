Qatar in context

0.04% vs 0.33%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Qatar is home to 0.04% of the world’s population and produces 0.33% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

+1,004%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 1,004% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (11.4 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

82 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Qatar was responsible for 82.2 Mt in 2023, 31% less than the 119.5 Mt produced at home: part of its output is consumed elsewhere.

192 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Qatar came with about 192 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a cleaner energy mix than the world average.

Its economy emitted 0.29 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), close to the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Qatar emit?

Qatar emitted 125.8 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.3% of global emissions. That is about 4 tonnes every second.

What are Qatar’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Qatar emits 41.3 tonnes per person, 8.7× the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (3.0 million people in 2024).

Are Qatar’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 5.3% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 164%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Qatar?

Gas is the largest source, at 82% of Qatar’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (102.8 million tonnes).

What are Qatar’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 82.2 million tonnes in 2023, 31% less than the 119.5 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because part of what it produces is exported and consumed in other countries. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Qatar contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Qatar has emitted about 3 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.1% of the world total, the 62nd largest historical contribution.
