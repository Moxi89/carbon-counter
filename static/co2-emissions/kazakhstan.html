Kazakhstan in context

0.25% vs 0.74%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Kazakhstan is home to 0.25% of the world’s population and produces 0.74% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

+7%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 7% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (267.4 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

213 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Kazakhstan was responsible for 212.7 Mt in 2023, 26% less than the 286.2 Mt produced at home: part of its output is consumed elsewhere.

333 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Kazakhstan came with about 333 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

0.55 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.55 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 1.9× the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Kazakhstan emit?

Kazakhstan emitted 287.0 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.7% of global emissions. That is about 9 tonnes every second.

What are Kazakhstan’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Kazakhstan emits 13.9 tonnes per person, 2.9× the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (20.6 million people in 2024).

Are Kazakhstan’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions were roughly flat in 2024 (+0.3% on 2023). Compared with 2005, emissions are up 45%. Emissions peaked in 2018 at 329.6 million tonnes and are now 13% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Kazakhstan?

Coal is the largest source, at 56% of Kazakhstan’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (160.2 million tonnes).

What are Kazakhstan’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 212.7 million tonnes in 2023, 26% less than the 286.2 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because part of what it produces is exported and consumed in other countries. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Kazakhstan contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Kazakhstan has emitted about 15 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.8% of the world total, the 22nd largest historical contribution.
