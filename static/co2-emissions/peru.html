Peru in context

0.42% vs 0.18%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Peru is home to 0.42% of the world’s population and produces 0.18% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+228%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 228% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (21.4 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

79 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Peru was responsible for 78.9 Mt in 2023, 19% more than the 66.1 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

188 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Peru came with about 188 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a cleaner energy mix than the world average.

Its economy emitted 0.19 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 34% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Peru emit?

Peru emitted 70.2 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.2% of global emissions. That is about 2 tonnes every second.

What are Peru’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Peru emits 2.0 tonnes per person, 57% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (34.2 million people in 2024).

Are Peru’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 6.2% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 132%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Peru?

Oil is the largest source, at 56% of Peru’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (39.1 million tonnes).

What are Peru’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 78.9 million tonnes in 2023, 19% more than the 66.1 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Peru contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Peru has emitted about 2 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.1% of the world total, the 66th largest historical contribution.
