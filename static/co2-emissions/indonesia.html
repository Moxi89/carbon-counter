Indonesia in context

3.5% vs 2.1%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Indonesia is home to 3.5% of the world’s population and produces 2.1% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+424%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 424% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (155.0 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

728 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Indonesia was responsible for 728.2 Mt in 2023, 4% less than the 762.4 Mt produced at home: part of its output is consumed elsewhere.

272 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Indonesia came with about 272 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

0.22 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.22 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 25% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Indonesia emit?

Indonesia emitted 812.2 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 2.1% of global emissions. That is about 26 tonnes every second.

What are Indonesia’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Indonesia emits 2.9 tonnes per person, 39% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (283.5 million people in 2024).

Are Indonesia’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 6.5% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 134%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Indonesia?

Coal is the largest source, at 54% of Indonesia’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (436.1 million tonnes).

What are Indonesia’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 728.2 million tonnes in 2023, 4% less than the 762.4 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because part of what it produces is exported and consumed in other countries. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Indonesia contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Indonesia has emitted about 17 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.9% of the world total, the 20th largest historical contribution.
