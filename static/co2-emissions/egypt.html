Egypt in context

1.4% vs 0.67%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Egypt is home to 1.4% of the world’s population and produces 0.67% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+254%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 254% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (72.9 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

253 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Egypt was responsible for 253.4 Mt in 2023, about the same as the 250.2 Mt produced within its borders.

230 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Egypt came with about 230 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to an energy mix close to the world average.

0.17 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.17 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 41% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Egypt emit?

Egypt emitted 258.4 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.7% of global emissions. That is about 8 tonnes every second.

What are Egypt’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Egypt emits 2.2 tonnes per person, 53% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (116.5 million people in 2024).

Are Egypt’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 3.3% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 55%. Emissions peaked in 2021 at 266.8 million tonnes and are now 3% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Egypt?

Oil is the largest source, at 44% of Egypt’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (113.0 million tonnes).

What are Egypt’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 253.4 million tonnes in 2023, roughly the same as its production-based emissions of 250.2 million tonnes. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Egypt contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Egypt has emitted about 7 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.4% of the world total, the 32nd largest historical contribution.
