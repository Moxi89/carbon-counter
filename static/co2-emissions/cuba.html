Cuba in context

0.13% vs 0.06%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Cuba is home to 0.13% of the world’s population and produces 0.06% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

−18%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 18% lower than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (29.6 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

290 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Cuba came with about 290 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

Its economy emitted 0.28 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), close to the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Cuba emit?

Cuba emitted 24.4 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.1% of global emissions. That is about 1 tonnes every second.

What are Cuba’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Cuba emits 2.2 tonnes per person, 53% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (11.0 million people in 2024).

Are Cuba’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 3.8% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are down 5%. Emissions peaked in 1989 at 35.7 million tonnes and are now 32% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Cuba?

Oil is the largest source, at 90% of Cuba’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (22.0 million tonnes).

How much has Cuba contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Cuba has emitted about 2 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.1% of the world total, the 71st largest historical contribution.
