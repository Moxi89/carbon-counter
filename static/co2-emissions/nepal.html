Nepal in context

0.36% vs 0.05%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Nepal is home to 0.36% of the world’s population and produces 0.05% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+2,586%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 2,586% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (0.7 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

47 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Nepal was responsible for 47.3 Mt in 2023, 158% more than the 18.3 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

356 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Nepal came with about 356 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

0.18 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.18 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 36% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Nepal emit?

Nepal emitted 18.8 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.1% of global emissions. That is about 1 tonnes every second.

What are Nepal’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Nepal emits 0.6 tonnes per person, 87% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (29.7 million people in 2024).

Are Nepal’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 2.7% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 527%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Nepal?

Oil is the largest source, at 45% of Nepal’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (8.5 million tonnes).

What are Nepal’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 47.3 million tonnes in 2023, 158% more than the 18.3 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Nepal contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Nepal has emitted about 0 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.0% of the world total, the 126th largest historical contribution.
