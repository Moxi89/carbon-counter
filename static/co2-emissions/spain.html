Spain in context

0.59% vs 0.57%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Spain is home to 0.59% of the world’s population and produces 0.57% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

−4%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 4% lower than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (229.4 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

261 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Spain was responsible for 260.6 Mt in 2023, 21% more than the 215.5 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

136 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Spain came with about 136 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much cleaner energy mix than the world average.

Its economy emitted 0.14 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 50% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Spain emit?

Spain emitted 220.3 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.6% of global emissions. That is about 7 tonnes every second.

What are Spain’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Spain emits 4.6 tonnes per person, close to the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (47.9 million people in 2024).

Are Spain’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 2.2% in 2024 compared with 2023. Emissions peaked in 2005 at 367.4 million tonnes and are now 40% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Spain?

Oil is the largest source, at 63% of Spain’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (139.5 million tonnes).

What are Spain’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 260.6 million tonnes in 2023, 21% more than the 215.5 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Spain contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Spain has emitted about 16 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.8% of the world total, the 21st largest historical contribution.
