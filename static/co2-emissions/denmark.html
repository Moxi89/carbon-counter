Denmark in context

0.07% vs 0.07%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

Denmark is home to 0.07% of the world’s population and produces 0.07% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

−47%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 47% lower than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (53.5 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

50 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, Denmark was responsible for 49.5 Mt in 2023, 72% more than the 28.8 Mt produced at home: it is a net importer of emissions.

145 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in Denmark came with about 145 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much cleaner energy mix than the world average.

0.10 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.10 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 66% below the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does Denmark emit?

Denmark emitted 28.4 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 0.1% of global emissions. That is about 1 tonnes every second.

What are Denmark’s CO₂ emissions per person?

Denmark emits 4.8 tonnes per person, close to the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (6.0 million people in 2024).

Are Denmark’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions fell 1.4% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are down 45%. Emissions peaked in 1996 at 74.9 million tonnes and are now 62% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in Denmark?

Oil is the largest source, at 76% of Denmark’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (21.5 million tonnes).

What are Denmark’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 49.5 million tonnes in 2023, 72% more than the 28.8 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because it imports more carbon-intensive goods than it exports. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has Denmark contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, Denmark has emitted about 4 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 0.2% of the world total, the 47th largest historical contribution.
