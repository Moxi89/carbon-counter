South Africa in context

0.78% vs 1.1%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

South Africa is home to 0.78% of the world’s population and produces 1.1% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is larger than its share of people.

+41%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 41% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (312.9 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

325 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, South Africa was responsible for 325.4 Mt in 2023, 25% less than the 436.6 Mt produced at home: part of its output is consumed elsewhere.

323 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in South Africa came with about 323 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a much more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

0.59 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.59 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), 2.1× the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does South Africa emit?

South Africa emitted 439.8 million tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 1.1% of global emissions. That is about 14 tonnes every second.

What are South Africa’s CO₂ emissions per person?

South Africa emits 6.9 tonnes per person, 1.5× the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (64.0 million people in 2024).

Are South Africa’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 0.7% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 6%. Emissions peaked in 2008 at 494.7 million tonnes and are now 11% lower.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in South Africa?

Coal is the largest source, at 84% of South Africa’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (371.4 million tonnes).

What are South Africa’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 325.4 million tonnes in 2023, 25% less than the 436.6 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because part of what it produces is exported and consumed in other countries. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has South Africa contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, South Africa has emitted about 23 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 1.2% of the world total, the 13th largest historical contribution.
