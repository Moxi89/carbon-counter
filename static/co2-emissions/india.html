India in context

17.8% vs 8.3%

Share of people vs share of CO₂

India is home to 17.8% of the world’s population and produces 8.3% of its fossil CO₂, so its share of emissions is smaller than its share of people.

+453%

Change since 1990

Emissions in 2024 were 453% higher than in 1990, the baseline year of the Kyoto Protocol (578.0 Mt then). World emissions rose 70% over the same period.

2,543 Mt

Consumption-based CO₂ (2023)

Counting the CO₂ in goods it imports and exports, India was responsible for 2,543.2 Mt in 2023, 17% less than the 3,062.8 Mt produced at home: part of its output is consumed elsewhere.

282 g

CO₂ per kWh of energy

Each kilowatt-hour of primary energy used in India came with about 282 g of CO₂ in 2024 (world: 218 g), pointing to a more fossil-heavy energy mix than the world average.

0.27 kg

CO₂ per dollar of GDP

Its economy emitted 0.27 kg of CO₂ for each dollar of output in 2022 (adjusted for purchasing power), close to the world average of 0.29 kg.

Questions and answers

How much CO₂ does India emit?

India emitted 3.19 billion tonnes of CO₂ from fossil fuels and industry in 2024, 8.3% of global emissions. That is about 101 tonnes every second.

What are India’s CO₂ emissions per person?

India emits 2.2 tonnes per person, 53% below the world average of 4.7 t. Per-person emissions divide the national total by population (1,450.9 million people in 2024).

Are India’s CO₂ emissions rising or falling?

Emissions rose 4.3% in 2024 compared with 2023. Compared with 2005, emissions are up 167%. 2024 was the highest year on record.

What is the main source of CO₂ emissions in India?

Coal is the largest source, at 66% of India’s fossil and industry CO₂ in 2024 (2.11 billion tonnes).

What are India’s consumption-based CO₂ emissions?

About 2,543.2 million tonnes in 2023, 17% less than the 3,062.8 million tonnes emitted within its borders, because part of what it produces is exported and consumed in other countries. Consumption-based accounting adds the emissions embodied in imports and subtracts those in exports.

How much has India contributed to historical CO₂ emissions?

Since 1750, India has emitted about 66 billion tonnes of fossil CO₂, 3.6% of the world total, the 7th largest historical contribution.
